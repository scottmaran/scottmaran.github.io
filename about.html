I grew up in New Jersey, playing just about every sport I could. Hockey was the one that stuck. My dad introduced me to the game, and watching it turned into reading everything I could find about it.

When hockey started having its own Moneyball moment, I wanted to understand the numbers. In high school, I wrote a fantasy hockey column at DobberHockey, published my own research, and presented work on special teams at the first Seattle Hockey Analytics Conference.

Following the questions

At Stanford, the challenge of collecting sports data led me toward computer vision and AI research with Itai Ashlagi and Fei-Fei Li. I studied pure mathematics and low-level computer science, finishing with a bachelor’s in mathematics and a master’s in statistics.

Football came next. The NFL’s Big Data Bowl opened up player-tracking data and gave me a new set of questions to work on. That research eventually led to an internship with the Arizona Cardinals.

Then I joined Swish Analytics. For nearly two years, I priced player props, applying statistics to markets where a model’s answers have to survive contact with an actual price.

What I’m thinking about now

These days, I work on prediction markets. I’m also interested in AI agents and the tools that make their behavior easier to understand. Lux is one of those tools.

I keep coming back to problems where data can help explain what’s happening, and where building something is a good way to find out.

If you’re thinking about AI, markets, sports, or an interesting question of your own, I’d like to hear about it.

Always happy to compare notes.
