Pay Attention
to Tackles.

Measuring tackling in the context of the opportunity.

A tackle count tells you who finished the play. This project asks who we should have expected to finish it, given where everyone was and how the play developed.

Read the submission ↗View source ↗

The question

Some tackles are much harder to make than others. A defender’s total depends on both their ability and the opportunities they get: positioning, teammates, blockers, and the ball carrier all matter.

For the 2024 NFL Big Data Bowl, I used player-tracking data to estimate the probability that each defender would make the tackle. That creates a basis for comparing what happened with what the situation suggested should happen.

Modeling the whole field

The model uses attention across all 23 field entities: the players and the ball. That lets it account for relationships across the field without imposing an arbitrary order on the players.

Learned positional embeddings add information about player roles. A transformer encoder combines that information with player inputs to produce tackle probabilities.

From probability to performance

Those probabilities become the baseline for Tackles over Expected: comparing actual tackles with the opportunities the model assigns to a defender. This puts performance in the context of the play.

The project also explores the structure learned by the model. The player embeddings reveal similarities in how players behave, with football’s positional relationships emerging from the data.
